What these numbers don't say.

An evaluator would find these on their own. We'd rather you read them here first.

The Sharpe edge isn't significant
t = 1.47 vs the S&P over 236 months. Reaching t = 2 would take roughly 17 more years of data.
The drawdown is a different claim
−16.2% against −50.7% isn't an estimate — it's the worst case that actually happened.
“A third of the drawdown” leans on 2008
The ratio is 0.32 over the full record and 0.68 from 2009. Our drawdown stays near −16% in all 8 windows tested; the index's doesn't.
The Sharpe doesn't survive deflation
Tested once, it would clear the bar (99.9%). After 344 trials on the same data, the probability falls to 81.1%.
Returns have fat tails
Excess kurtosis +2.52 — more extreme months than the Sharpe ratio assumes. Skew is near zero; the index's is −0.71.
Cash earns T-bills, not zero
Every ratio uses the real risk-free rate (FRED TB3MS, 1.55% a year on average). With rf = 0 the Sharpe would read 1.01.
